C07 · 06–09 min

Place the key where the multiplicity lives

A session belongs to one conference. Its conferenceId is on the many side. A one-to-one relationship also needs uniqueness; an arrow in a diagram cannot enforce it.

Conference(id) 1 ── * Session(id, conferenceId)
One-to-one: foreign key + uniqueness
Optionality: a separate nullability decision

What prevents two rows from claiming the same one-to-one partner?

The appropriate unique constraint, in addition to the foreign key. Foreign-key actions such as restrict, cascade or set-null express different lifecycle policies; choose them from ownership.

U07 lecture §§1–3,6,11–12 · Source expectations and models are not current database evidence.

Three different constraint decisions

A foreign key checks that an admitted reference points to an owner. NOT NULL decides whether the reference can be absent. UNIQUE decides whether two rows can claim the same relationship. In a many-to-one relation several child rows may reference the same owner; a one-to-one design adds appropriate uniqueness. Neutral demonstration 01 attempts all three refusals independently, with foreign-key enforcement enabled.

C07 · 09–12 min

The membership has facts of its own

An attendee can register for different sessions with different ticket types. The junction is a domain record, not merely hidden ORM plumbing.

Registration(sessionId, attendeeId, ticketType)
UNIQUE(sessionId, attendeeId)

It belongs to one membership. A surrogate registration ID does not automatically prevent duplicate session/attendee pairs. The composite constraint expresses that business identity.
