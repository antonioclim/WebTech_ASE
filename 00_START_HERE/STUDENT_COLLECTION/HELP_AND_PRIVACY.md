# Help, privacy and submission

If a step fails, report the object ID, selected edition, exact command, project folder and full error. Include the relevant redacted verification output. Keep your work and the failing result. Ask the lecturer for the permitted continuation when an installation, account, AI tool or required service is unavailable.

Keep passwords, access tokens, session cookies, recovery codes and private browser tabs out of screenshots, saved prompts and submissions. Capture only the application and evidence required by the guide. Inspect files before uploading them to Moodle or an AI service. Optional JSON drafts and diagnostic archives stay local unless the lecturer asks for them privately; they are not the default Moodle submission.

If an AI tool or account is unavailable, request the lecturer's permitted alternative. A supplied trace is an observation source, not evidence that you held a real conversation. Label supplied material, your own reasoning, observed results, unexecuted commands and uncertainty accurately. This repository does not establish an institution-wide AI policy. Day 0 account acknowledgement flags are your statements; they do not verify a login, 2FA or an authentic AI exchange.

## Submit your seminar evidence

Use the form linked by the selected seminar entry and follow that seminar's required evidence and filename rules. Complete your own evidence, export the final form to PDF and review every page for readability, missing fields and private information. Upload the PDF privately to the lecturer's matching Assignment on `online.ase.ro`, check that the uploaded file opens, and use the final Submit action when available. Keep your local copy and any submission receipt. Uploading a draft is not automatically final submission.

Current deadlines, marks, late-work rules, permitted AI alternatives and account decisions belong to the lecturer and institution. Public rubrics explain a package's proposed evidence structure; they do not create a deadline, grade or configured Moodle Assignment. Course demonstrations do not create an additional course upload unless the lecturer explicitly requires one.

## Terms used in the packages

| Term | What to do with it |
| --- | --- |
| Package identity or SHA-256 | Check that you obtained the intended files; it does not prove your solution or submission. |
| Baseline check | Check starter infrastructure before judging the assessed change. |
| Objective check | Check the required behaviour; failure can be intentional before you implement it. |
| Regression check | Check that your change preserved existing behaviour. |
| Initial-state check | Confirm the supplied starter has its expected successful and unfinished checks. |
| Allowed-edit boundary | Change only the files the guide permits; keep protected tests and manifests intact. |
| Guided observation | Run/read the supplied demonstration and record what you actually observed; it may not require implementing the whole example. |
| Optional extension | Additional practice unless the guide or lecturer explicitly makes it required. |
| ADR | An architecture decision record: state the decision, evidence, tradeoffs and limitation requested by the guide. |
| Evidence | A reproducible observation tied to your claim, method and limitation. |
| Preview, phase or FINAL_LOCAL | Creation-time package status; it does not establish current runtime, native platform or institutional acceptance. |

Historical source copies, PREVIEW archives and original dependency lockfiles remain useful comparison evidence. Use the selected entry and required project route for class work. A retained historical dependency graph is not a fresh security/advisory result for the newly selected package.

Reading the local guide and filling a local form work offline. Acquiring missing dependencies, accessing permitted online accounts and submitting to Moodle require connectivity. Preserve progress and follow the guide or lecturer's permitted continuation; do not manufacture a successful output or AI dialogue.
