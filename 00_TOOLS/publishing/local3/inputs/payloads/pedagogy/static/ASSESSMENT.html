What evidence can show

  • Correctness: the specified positive and negative cases, exact commands and actual outputs.
  • Explanation: why your approach meets the contract and how the task's boundary works.
  • Investigation: a prediction, observed result and reasoned response to a failure or counterexample.
  • Independence: your own work, with AI suggestions checked against source and tests.
  • Reporting: traceable actual observations, limitations and a readable complete PDF.

These are qualitative review criteria. Mark weights, deadlines, late rules, permitted alternatives and institutional policy come from the lecturer's actual Assignment. This archive invents none of them.

Plan every required task and finish with a recap

Prepare the runtime and expressly required dependencies before class. Use 5 planned minutes to check the prepared environment, 30–45 for each required microproject, 15 for one shared genuine bounded AI critique and independent check, 5 to review and save the single PDF, then 5 for a final recap. That gives 90–120 planned minutes for two projects or 120–165 for three. These are unpiloted planning ranges, not measured completion times or empirical minimums.

For each project, use about 5 planned minutes to read the contract and record a prediction, 20–30 to implement, run checks and investigate results, then 5–10 to review a counterexample and record evidence. These checkpoints total 30–45 planned minutes. Preserve real failures and changes to your reasoning.

If your actual institutional slot is shorter than these planned blocks, confirm the continuation arrangement with your lecturer. Every listed task remains required individual classroom work. Keep unfinished work marked unfinished; the end of a meeting does not make it complete.

In your final recap, connect what you achieved to the mechanism you learned, explain why your evidence supports that conclusion and name the next application or boundary to investigate. Use your actual results, including failures and limits, rather than a prepared success statement.

Finish the recap before actual submission. If it leads you to add or change evidence, update the form, renew the affected declarations and export and review the latest single PDF before uploading it. Use the agreed continuation arrangement if more time is needed; do not submit an earlier PDF as the updated record.

When something is blocked

If the required AI service or account is unavailable or disallowed, record BLOCKED, the cause and the affected activity, then contact the lecturer through the authorised course channel for an alternative. Keep completing independent code and observations where possible. Do not fabricate an AI exchange or treat BLOCKED as an automatic exemption or a grade.

For failed tests or installation, preserve the command, useful output and your investigation. Distinguish unexecuted work from a failed execution. A sample fixture is teaching material, not personal submission evidence.
